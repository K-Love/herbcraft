Marshmallow Root

Althaea officinalis

Key Facts

Scientific Name
Althaea officinalis
Plant Family
Malvaceae (Mallow family)
Origin
Native to Europe, western Asia, and North Africa
Primary Use
Digestive & Respiratory Soothing

Overview

Marshmallow root is a perennial herb whose root contains high levels of mucilage, a gel-like substance that soothes and protects irritated mucous membranes. It has been used medicinally since ancient Egyptian and Greek times.

Traditional Uses

Used in ancient Egypt, Greece, and Rome for sore throats, coughs, digestive inflammation, and skin conditions. The original marshmallow candy was made from the root's mucilage. Used in European folk medicine for urinary tract irritation and wound healing.

Active Compounds

Mucilage polysaccharides (primarily rhamnogalacturonan I), flavonoids (quercetin, kaempferol), phenolic acids, and pectin. The mucilage forms a protective coating on mucous membranes.

Potential Benefits — Unverified Summary

Soothes irritated mucous membranes in the throat, digestive tract, and urinary system. May relieve dry cough, sore throat, gastritis, and IBS symptoms. Topically, may help with wound healing and skin inflammation. Diuretic properties support urinary tract health.

Safety & Side Effects

Generally very safe with minimal side effects. May slow absorption of oral medications; take 1-2 hours apart from other drugs. May lower blood sugar; monitor if diabetic. Avoid during pregnancy (insufficient safety data). May cause allergic reactions in people sensitive to Malvaceae plants.

When to See a Doctor

Seek immediate medical attention if you experience:

  • Severe allergic reactions (difficulty breathing, swelling of face or throat, severe rash)
  • Chest pain or irregular heartbeat
  • Severe digestive issues (persistent vomiting, bloody stools)
  • Severe headache or dizziness
  • Any symptoms that worsen or do not improve

Always consult a healthcare professional before using herbal supplements if you:

  • Are pregnant, nursing, or planning to become pregnant
  • Are taking prescription medications
  • Have a chronic health condition
  • Are scheduled for surgery
  • Are giving herbs to children
