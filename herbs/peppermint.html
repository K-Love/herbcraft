Peppermint

Mentha × piperita

Key Facts

Scientific Name
Mentha × piperita [2]
Plant Family
Lamiaceae [2]
Origin
Europe and the Middle East [2]
Primary Use
Culinary use and digestive research [1]

Overview

Peppermint is a hybrid of watermint and spearmint. Its leaves and concentrated essential oil are different preparations and should not be treated as interchangeable. [1] [2]

Traditional Uses

Mint plants have historically been used for digestive complaints. This history does not establish a treatment benefit. [1]

Preparation Matters

Peppermint oil contains menthol. Evidence from enteric-coated oil capsules does not establish the same effects for peppermint leaf tea. [1]

What the Evidence Says

Limited research suggests enteric-coated peppermint-oil capsules may improve IBS symptoms in adults. There is insufficient evidence to establish health benefits from peppermint leaf. Peppermint oil alone may worsen indigestion in some people. [1]

Safety & Side Effects

Oral peppermint oil can cause heartburn, nausea, abdominal pain, and occasionally allergic reactions. Skin use may cause irritation. Do not apply it to an infant’s or young child’s face or let them inhale menthol. Medicinal-dose safety in pregnancy and breastfeeding is uncertain. Discuss use with a clinician if you take medicines. [1]

When to See a Doctor

Seek immediate medical attention if you experience:

  • Severe allergic reactions (difficulty breathing, swelling of face or throat, severe rash)
  • Chest pain or irregular heartbeat
  • Severe digestive issues (persistent vomiting, bloody stools)
  • Severe headache or dizziness
  • Any symptoms that worsen or do not improve

Always consult a healthcare professional before using herbal supplements if you:

  • Are pregnant, nursing, or planning to become pregnant
  • Are taking prescription medications
  • Have a chronic health condition
  • Are scheduled for surgery
  • Are giving herbs to children

Growing Notes

Peppermint prefers rich, moist soil and partial shade. It spreads aggressively by rhizomes; a container helps manage its spread. Pinching shoot tips encourages bushier growth. [2]
