Overview

Calendula (pot marigold) is a flowering plant in the daisy family, widely used in herbal medicine for its anti-inflammatory and wound-healing properties, particularly in topical applications.

Traditional Uses

Used in traditional European and Ayurvedic medicine for centuries to treat skin wounds, burns, rashes, and infections. Also used internally for digestive and menstrual complaints.

Active Compounds

Contains triterpenoids (oleanolic acid glycosides), flavonoids (quercetin, isorhamnetin), carotenoids, and polysaccharides.

Potential Benefits — Unverified Summary

Highly effective for wound healing, reducing skin inflammation, and treating dermatitis and eczema. May also have antifungal and antimicrobial properties.

Safety & Side Effects

Generally safe for topical use. May cause allergic reactions in people sensitive to ragweed or other Asteraceae plants. Avoid during pregnancy (may stimulate uterine contractions). Limited safety data for internal use.

When to See a Doctor

Seek immediate medical attention if you experience:

  • Severe allergic reactions (difficulty breathing, swelling of face or throat, severe rash)
  • Chest pain or irregular heartbeat
  • Severe digestive issues (persistent vomiting, bloody stools)
  • Severe headache or dizziness
  • Any symptoms that worsen or do not improve

Always consult a healthcare professional before using herbal supplements if you:

  • Are pregnant, nursing, or planning to become pregnant
  • Are taking prescription medications
  • Have a chronic health condition
  • Are scheduled for surgery
  • Are giving herbs to children
